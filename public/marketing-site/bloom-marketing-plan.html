Bloom Business School: 12-Month Marketing Plan

Get more qualified applicants, raise enrollment, and build Bloom into a trusted name in practical business education.

Before you use this plan: it is built on common assumptions for a private business school. Bloom offers diploma, MBA/mini-MBA, and short professional courses to fresh graduates, working professionals, and companies. The numbers marked [target] are starting points. Swap in Bloom's real enrollment figures, prices, and budget.

1. Executive summary

Bloom will present itself as "the business school that makes you job-ready, fast". It offers practical, career-focused programs taught by working industry experts, with flexible schedules. Over the next 12 months the plan focuses on three things:

  1. Awareness: build a recognizable brand through content, social media, and alumni stories.
  2. Lead generation: fill the funnel with qualified leads through paid ads, free workshops, and partnerships.
  3. Conversion: turn leads into enrolled students with fast follow-up, consultations, and flexible payment options.

2. Situation analysis (SWOT)

Strengths

  • Practical, career-focused curriculum
  • Instructors who work in industry
  • Flexible formats (evening, weekend, online)
  • Smaller classes and personal attention

Weaknesses

  • Brand awareness is still low
  • Few published alumni outcomes so far
  • Smaller budget than universities
  • Accreditation may be limited or still in progress

Opportunities

  • Growing demand for upskilling and career switching
  • Companies buying training for their teams
  • Short, stackable certificates are popular
  • Short-form video and LinkedIn reach are cheap

Threats

  • Universities with established MBA brands
  • Low-cost online platforms (Coursera, Udemy, etc.)
  • Economic pressure on tuition budgets
  • Price wars from other training centers

4. Positioning and messaging

Positioning statement: For ambitious graduates and professionals who want real career progress, Bloom Business School offers practical business programs taught by industry experts. Unlike traditional universities, Bloom is flexible, affordable, and focused on skills you can use at work the next day.

Tagline options: "Grow your career. Bloom." · "Learn it today. Use it tomorrow." · "Where careers bloom."

Key messages:

  • Practical: real case studies, projects, and tools instead of theory alone.
  • Expert-led: taught by managers and founders who do this work every day.
  • Flexible: evening, weekend, and online options.
  • Outcomes: career support, networking, and a certificate employers recognize.

Brand voice: encouraging, clear, and confident. Avoid academic jargon, and lead with student results.

6. Channel strategy

6.1 Website and SEO

  • Give every program its own landing page with outcomes, curriculum, instructors, schedule, price or installments, FAQs, and one clear call to action ("Book a free consultation").
  • Put a WhatsApp button and a short lead form on every page.
  • Publish two blog posts a month that answer the questions students search for, such as "Is a mini-MBA worth it?", "How to move into management", and "Best courses for marketing careers".
  • Set up and optimize the Google Business Profile, and collect reviews after every course.

6.2 Social media

  • LinkedIn: for professionals and B2B buyers. Post instructor insights, alumni wins, and corporate case studies 3× a week.
  • Instagram and TikTok: for graduates. Post short "career tips" reels, day-in-class clips, and student stories 4–5× a week.
  • Facebook: for course announcements, events, and community groups. Post 3× a week.
  • YouTube: long-form webinars, recorded masterclasses, and full testimonials.

6.3 Paid advertising

  • Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram): lead-form and WhatsApp campaigns for each program, plus retargeting of website visitors.
  • Google Search: high-intent keywords such as "MBA courses", "HR diploma", and "digital marketing course" near the school's locations.
  • LinkedIn ads: Executive and B2B programs only, because LinkedIn costs more per click.
  • A/B test at least two creatives and two offers for each campaign every month.

6.4 Events and free workshops

  • Run a free monthly online masterclass for each program area. It works as a top-of-funnel lead magnet.
  • Hold a quarterly open day on campus: meet instructors, attend a sample class, and get an enrollment discount on the day.
  • Hold career talks at universities and student activity clubs.

6.5 Partnerships

  • Universities and student unions: graduate discounts and career-day booths.
  • Companies: corporate training packages and group discounts for employees.
  • Professional bodies and chambers of commerce: co-branded events.
  • Influencers and career coaches: micro-influencers (10k–100k followers) with affiliate codes.

6.6 Email, WhatsApp and CRM

  • Log every lead in a CRM (for example HubSpot or Zoho) with its source tracked.
  • Contact every new lead by WhatsApp or phone within 1 hour.
  • Run an automated nurture sequence: welcome, program guide, alumni story, invitation to a free class, then a deadline reminder.
  • Send a monthly newsletter to leads and alumni.

6.7 Referrals and alumni

  • Referral program: a discount for both the current student and the friend they refer.
  • An alumni ambassador program where graduates share their story and speak at events.
  • Collect a short video testimonial at the end of every cohort.

8. Offers and pricing tactics

  • Early-bird discount (for example 15%) up to 3 weeks before each cohort starts.
  • Installment plans that remove the price barrier.
  • Group and company discounts for 3 or more people.
  • Merit or need-based scholarships (a few per cohort) that also generate PR.
  • Bundles: stack short courses into a diploma at a lower total price.

12. Team and responsibilities

  • Marketing manager: owns the plan, budget, and KPIs.
  • Content creator / social media specialist: daily content and community management.
  • Performance marketer (in-house or agency): ads, tracking, and optimization.
  • Admissions / sales team: lead follow-up, consultations, and enrollment.
  • Designer / videographer (freelance is fine): creative assets.

13. Measurement and reporting

  • Weekly: leads by source, cost per lead, response time, and consultations booked.
  • Monthly: enrollments, conversion rate, CAC by channel, social growth, and top content.
  • Quarterly: marketing ROI (tuition revenue ÷ marketing spend), budget reallocation, and a review of the plan.
  • Put UTM links on every campaign and record a "How did you hear about us?" answer for every student who enrolls.
